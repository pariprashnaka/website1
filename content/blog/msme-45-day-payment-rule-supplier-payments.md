Many Indian businesses run on supplier credit. You take the goods, pay in 60 or 90 days, and use the gap to fund your own sales. For purchases from micro and small suppliers, that habit now has a tax cost.

Since 1 April 2024, payments to micro and small enterprises must meet the deadlines of the MSMED Act, or the expense cannot be deducted until you actually pay. This guide explains the rule and how to stay inside it.

## The rule in plain language

Section 15 of the MSMED Act sets the payment deadline for buying goods or services from a micro or small enterprise:

- **No written agreement:** pay within **15 days** of accepting the goods or services.
- **Written agreement:** pay by the agreed date, which **cannot exceed 45 days**.

The tax consequence was added by the Finance Act 2023, effective 1 April 2024. An amount owed to a micro or small enterprise is deductible only if it is paid within those timelines. If it is still unpaid on 31 March, the expense is disallowed for that year and added back to your taxable income. The deduction becomes available in the year you actually pay.

Late payment also attracts **compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate**, with monthly rests, and that interest is not tax-deductible.

## Which suppliers are covered

Only **micro and small** enterprises. Medium enterprises are excluded. The way to know a supplier's status is their Udyam Registration Certificate. The current classification limits, applicable from 1 April 2025, are:

- **Micro:** investment up to ₹2.5 crore and turnover up to ₹10 crore
- **Small:** investment up to ₹25 crore and turnover up to ₹100 crore
- **Medium:** investment up to ₹125 crore and turnover up to ₹500 crore

Both investment and turnover limits must be met, and export turnover is excluded.

## What changed in 2026

Two developments are worth knowing:

1. **A new Income-tax Act.** The Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on 1 April 2026. From tax year 2026-27, the equivalent provision is Section 37(2)(g). Section 43B(h) still applies to earlier years under the transition rules.
2. **A stronger MSME law.** The MSME (Development) Amendment Act received assent on 13 August 2026, with each provision to take effect on a date notified separately. It speeds up dispute timelines for eligible suppliers, and repeated failure to disclose unpaid dues can attract a fine of up to ₹1 lakh.

## How to stay compliant without chasing invoices

1. **Tag every supplier.** Collect Udyam certificates and mark each vendor as micro, small, medium or other in your records.
2. **Put payment terms in writing.** Without an agreement the limit is 15 days, not 45.
3. **Track due dates per invoice.** Standard 60-day credit periods no longer work for covered suppliers.
4. **Review payables before 31 March.** This is the cut-off for the deduction in that year.
5. **Plan cash flow.** Faster payments to small suppliers have to be funded somewhere, so forecast them.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the MSME 45-day rule?**
Payments to micro and small suppliers must be made within 15 days, or within the agreed date up to 45 days where there is a written agreement, to claim the tax deduction in that year.

**Does it apply to medium-sized suppliers?**
No. Only micro and small enterprises are covered.

**What happens if I pay late?**
The expense is disallowed in the year it was unpaid and deductible when you pay. Compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate may also apply.

**Is the rule still in force under the new Income-tax Act?**
Yes. From tax year 2026-27 the equivalent provision is Section 37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
