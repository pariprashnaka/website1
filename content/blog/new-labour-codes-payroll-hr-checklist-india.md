On 21 November 2025, the Government of India brought four labour codes into force across the country. Together they replace 29 existing central labour laws. For a small employer this is not just a legal tidy-up. It changes how wages are defined, who qualifies for gratuity, what an employment contract must contain and when a business can reduce its workforce.

This guide covers the changes that matter most to payroll and HR, and what to do about them.

## The four codes

- The **Code on Wages, 2019**
- The **Code on Social Security, 2020**
- The **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020**
- The **Industrial Relations Code, 2020**

## What changes for a small employer

**One definition of wages.** The codes standardise what counts as wages. That matters because the definition feeds directly into provident fund, gratuity, leave encashment, statutory bonus and overtime. If your salary structure splits pay into many small allowances, it needs a fresh look. Gratuity calculated on the revised definition may also create retroactive cost, according to KPMG's analysis.

**Gratuity sooner for fixed-term staff.** A fixed-term employee becomes eligible for gratuity after **one year** of service, against five years for permanent employees.

**Appointment letters for everyone.** Every employee, including those in informal roles, must receive an appointment letter with specified details.

**Higher threshold for retrenchment.** Government approval for lay-offs, retrenchment or closure now applies to establishments with **300 workers**, up from 100.

**Women in night shifts.** Women may work night shifts in all establishments, subject to their consent and safety measures.

**Health check-ups and final pay.** Workers above a specified age are entitled to a free annual health check-up. Wages must be paid within two working days of termination.

## Where the uncertainty is

The codes took effect on 21 November 2025, but the supporting central and state rules were still being finalised in the reports we reviewed, with draft rules expected to be published for a 45-day consultation. Details such as overtime rates and the age for health check-ups depend on those rules. **Check the current status of the rules for your state before locking in payroll changes.**

## A payroll and HR checklist

1. **Re-run your pay structure.** Test every employee's pay against the new wage definition and see how PF, gratuity and leave encashment move.
2. **Model the cost.** EY recommends impact modelling across HR, finance, payroll and legal teams before the numbers surprise you at year-end.
3. **Issue or refresh appointment letters.** Include all required details, and keep a signed copy.
4. **Review fixed-term and contract arrangements.** The one-year gratuity rule and limits on contract labour for core activities change the maths of short contracts.
5. **Update HR policies.** Working hours, leave and exit procedures need to match the new text.
6. **Fix the data.** Attendance, leave balances and salary history should live in one system, not three, so recalculations are quick and defensible.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did the new labour codes come into force?**
On 21 November 2025, across the country.

**Which laws do they replace?**
Twenty-nine existing central labour laws, consolidated into four codes.

**Do fixed-term employees get gratuity?**
Yes, after one year of service instead of five.

**Do I need an appointment letter for every employee?**
Yes. The codes require appointment letters for all employees, with specified details.

**Are the rules final?**
The codes are in force, but supporting central and state rules were still being notified in the sources we reviewed. Confirm the latest position before acting.
