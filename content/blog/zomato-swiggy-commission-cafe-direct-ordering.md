Ask any café owner whether the delivery apps are worth it, and the honest answer is "they bring orders, but I am not sure I make money on them." That uncertainty is rational. The commission looks like one number on a statement, but it is only part of what an order costs.

## What restaurants report paying

Reporting by YourStory in 2025 put the picture together:

- Restaurants pay roughly **18% to 26% in fixed commission**, plus platform fees and GST. Small outlets sit at the higher end, while national chains negotiate lower.
- Rishav Jain of Alvarez & Marsal said larger brands pay **10% to 16%**, while smaller outlets typically pay **"upwards of 20%."**
- Restaurateurs told YourStory that commission, advertising and discovery fees together can make up **45% to 50% of total payouts** to the platforms.
- A Chennai-based quick-service chain reported its advertising spend on the platforms rose three times between 2019 and 2024.

So the headline commission understates the real cost, because visibility on the app increasingly has to be paid for on top.

## The math for one café

The figures below are an illustration, not a forecast. Assume an average order of ₹400 and a 20% commission, and ignore ads, platform fees and discounts, all of which push the real cost higher.

| Orders per month | Commission paid at 20% |
|---|---|
| 500 | ₹40,000 |
| 1,000 | ₹80,000 |
| 2,000 | ₹1,60,000 |

At 1,000 orders a month, that is ₹9.6 lakh a year paid out before a single rupee of advertising.

## The cost you cannot see: the customer

The commission is the visible loss. The invisible one is ownership of the relationship. A customer who orders through an aggregator belongs to the aggregator. You do not get their number to send a festival offer. You do not learn what they order on a rainy Tuesday. The next time they are hungry, the app decides whether to show them you or a competitor.

## What direct ordering changes

When customers order straight from your café for dine-in, takeaway or delivery:

- **You keep the margin.** There is no per-order commission to a third party.
- **You keep the customer.** Their details and order history are yours, handled under your own consent notice.
- **You can see everything.** For a multi-outlet owner, one dashboard shows live orders, sales and operations across every location.

New entrants are also pushing prices down. Rapido's Ownly food-delivery arm has said it will offer restaurants roughly half the commission of the large platforms, though it launched only in select parts of Bengaluru. Direct ordering is not a replacement for discovery apps, but it can move your regulars off them.

## A sensible way to start

You do not need to quit the apps. Use them to be discovered, and move repeat customers to your own channel:

1. Put a QR code on every table, bag and receipt that opens your own ordering page.
2. Offer regulars a small reason to order direct, such as a free add-on.
3. Track what share of orders comes from each channel, so the savings are measured, not assumed.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much commission do Zomato and Swiggy charge?**
Reported figures range from about 10% to 16% for large brands to upwards of 20% for smaller outlets, with an overall range of roughly 18% to 26% plus fees and GST.

**Is direct ordering cheaper?**
Per order, yes, since there is no marketplace commission. You still pay for your own ordering software and any payment fees, so compare the full cost.

**Should I leave the delivery apps?**
Not necessarily. Many cafés use them for discovery and push repeat customers to direct ordering.
